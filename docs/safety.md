# Safety boundaries

The repository emphasizes software, simulation and public datasets first. Safety guidance is about risk management, not a procedural manual for hazardous experimentation.

## Low-risk learning

Typical safe v1 work includes public datasets, synthetic signals, offline simulations, visualization, feature extraction and software-only closed-loop models. These can teach most of the conceptual and computational foundations without exposing a learner or participant to hardware risk.

## Activities requiring additional oversight

Human-subject data collection requires appropriate consent, privacy handling and institutional procedures. Clinical claims, patient-facing systems, implanted or otherwise invasive neural interfaces, and device validation belong in qualified research/clinical settings with the relevant institutional and regulatory controls.

Electrical or other physiological stimulation can cause injury, unintended physiological effects, measurement artifacts, or misleading results when performed without qualified design, monitoring and safeguards. The repository therefore does not provide DIY stimulation procedures.

## Personal neural data

Neural data can be sensitive even when the original task seems low-risk. Minimize collection, document provenance and permissions, and avoid uploading identifiable or private recordings to public issue trackers or repositories.

When a project crosses from analysis into physical intervention, stop treating it as a casual learning exercise and move to the appropriate supervised pathway.
