# I want to study neuroethics

Learners who want to analyze ethical and governance questions arising from neural sensing, decoding and intervention.

## Destination

- Define a concrete neurotechnology case before making a normative claim.
- Separate empirical findings, engineering assumptions and normative recommendations.
- Write a sourced policy brief that names stakeholders, trade-offs and uncertainty.

## Starting assumptions

- No legal training is required; the path teaches how to scope and source technical claims.

Produce a 1,500–2,000 word policy brief on one defined neurotechnology governance issue with primary or authoritative references and a stakeholder map.

## Deeper path

Compare governance approaches across jurisdictions or institutions and connect normative arguments to technical system choices.

## Common misconceptions

- Technical feasibility and ethical acceptability are different questions.
- Calling neural data “private” is not a complete analysis without examining purpose, inference, access and governance.

## Typical failure modes

- Writing broad moral claims without defining the technology, population, data, harm or governance mechanism.

## Research directions

- Mental privacy, agency in adaptive systems, neurotechnology access, workplace and education governance, disability justice and international governance.

## Safety

No special physical safety issue for the learning path; do not present legal commentary as jurisdiction-independent legal advice.

## Ethics

Analyze mental privacy, autonomy, agency, fairness, accessibility, consent and data governance with attributed evidence.

## What to learn next

Choose a concrete technical domain and revisit the ethical questions with domain-specific evidence.
